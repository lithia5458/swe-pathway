The floor

On a low-energy day, a session counts if you do 20 minutes of anything on the topic and make one commit. Watch a lecture, read docs, or tidy notes — that still moves you forward.

Slip rules

  • Missed a session: don't double up. Drop that week's stretch items and move on.
  • Stuck >15 min: write the question in your log, skip ahead, ask your senior engineer or an AI tutor later.
  • Behind a full week at a checkpoint: use the next flex week to catch up, or cut one stretch item from the next phase. Never skip a checkpoint — each one is a working thing you can show.

Flex weeks — no new material

  • Weeks 9–11: Winter break · Dec 21 – Jan 8 (3 weeks)
  • Week 23: Spring break · Mar 29 – Apr 3

Phase 10 · The project

wrench.repair — built by you, from scratch.

Four weeks, one vehicle system end-to-end. A user describes a problem and their experience level, and gets sourced, level-appropriate repair steps.

Expected architecture

Worker serves the page and answers questions. Python prepares manuals offline. Phase 6 is where you design this for real.

Weeks 26–27 · Scaffold + data

Turn the Phase 6 design doc into tasks, deploy a bare Worker via CI, run one vehicle / one system through your Phase 7 pipeline, embed & index it.

Week 28 · Retrieval + prompt

Test with 10 real questions, fix the worst chunking, design prompts with safety rules that send risky jobs to a professional.

Week 29 · Wire + evaluate

Form → API → Claude. Evaluate with 20 questions across beginner & experienced levels, check citations, deploy, retrospective.

Scope warning: Four weeks is tight for a first build. Stay with one vehicle system and cut features before cutting checkpoints — whatever isn't finished becomes your next roadmap. For anything safety-critical (brakes, airbags), be conservative and say when to see a professional. Checkpoint: one system answers end-to-end, every answer cites its source passage, each change merged through a PR.
